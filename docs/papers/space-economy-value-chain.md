# The space economy value chain: where a middle power can hold control

*Open Sovereign Research · public paper v1.0 · October 2026 · OGL-Canada-2.0*

## In brief

Every physical layer of the space stack — silicon, orbital infrastructure, frontier models — is largely controlled outside any single middle power, and no procurement decision changes that. What a nation *can* control is the layer between them: the **sovereign decision layer**, where data and supply are registered, screened and adjudicated under national jurisdiction. Its scarcity is legal, not physical. This paper explains why, maps it onto the Sovereign Seam, and lists the open questions.

## 1. The stack and its seam

Read the space economy as six layers: **applications, a decision layer, models, infrastructure, chips, energy**. Legal scholarship increasingly treats law itself as a layer of the AI ecosystem alongside energy, compute, cloud, models and applications.[1] The physical layers are capital-heavy and globally concentrated. The decision layer is not physical at all: it is who holds the record, who applies the rules and whose determination counts.

## 2. Why the seam is legal, not physical

- **Data is nonrival.** Many parties can use the same dataset at once without consuming it, so copies are cheap. Value depends on who owns the data and governs its use.[2] Returns to data rise during an accumulation window and flatten over the long run.[3]
- **Location is not control.** US legal process can reach data held by US-headquartered providers wherever it is stored.[4] The Government of Canada's own white paper states that as long as a cloud provider operating in Canada is subject to foreign law, "Canada will not have full sovereignty over its data."[5]
- **The policy frame exists, the coordination layer does not.** Canada's Defence Industrial Strategy names ten sovereign capabilities and adopts Build–Partner–Buy.[6][7] The federal list of key industrial capabilities, used to steer industrial-benefit obligations, contains no data or data-sovereignty capability.[8]

**Reading.** Siting a ground station or a data hall in-country addresses residency. Control is decided by jurisdiction over the operator and by who applies the determination logic.

## 3. Build, Partner, Buy across the stack

| Posture | Where it fits | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Build** | Where control is the point | The registry, screening and adjudication layer; a ground segment under national control; certification of the space workforce |
| **Partner** | Where plurality is the point | Frontier models, allied sensing, advanced and rad-hard silicon. Each is secured across several jurisdictions, never a single dependency |
| **Buy** | Where markets work, with conditions | Launch access, platforms and enterprise software procured competitively, with data rights in the contract |

These are framings for debate. Readings for individual cells are open to all contributors in [`seam/readings/`](../../seam/readings/).

## 4. Demand is arriving

- In March 2026 the Department of National Defence committed **$200 million** to a ten-year launch-pad lease near Canso, Nova Scotia, and **$105 million** to the *Launch the North* challenge.[9][10]
- Canada is the first non-EU/EEA partner in the EU's €150-billion SAFE defence instrument.[11]
- Sovereign compute has its own national strategy.[12]

Launch is the visible half of sovereign access to space. The other half is the ground segment that receives what launches, the registry that adjudicates the data, and the certified workforce that operates both. That half decides whether sovereign access produces sovereign capability or only sovereign hardware.

## 5. Open questions

1. Which of the 60 Seam cells can only be held **by construction** (Build), and which can be held **by contract** (Buy with conditions)?
2. What would an open, published competency schema for the space workforce look like? Existing open standards — CTDL, Open Badges 3.0, Verifiable Credentials 2.0 — suggest a path.[13][14][15]
3. Where is the space supply chain single-sourced or exposed to a single foreign jurisdiction? This question is the bridge to the sister project, OpenChokepoint.
4. What evidence would change a posture? Every reading should name its own flip condition.
